Work deadlines, money worries, family pressures, exams, traffic: modern life comes with plenty of stress. Short bursts of stress are normal and can even be useful. But when stress is constant, it affects your body in ways that can show up in the bedroom.

## How stress affects sexual health

When you're stressed, your body releases hormones such as adrenaline and cortisol that prepare you for action. This "fight or flight" state:

- tightens blood vessels, which can make erections harder to get and keep
- shifts attention towards threats and problems, away from pleasure and connection
- tenses muscles, including the pelvic floor, which can speed up ejaculation
- disrupts sleep, which lowers energy and testosterone
- can lower desire, simply because your mind is busy elsewhere

Long-term stress is also linked with high blood pressure, weight gain around the belly, and low mood, all of which affect sexual health over time. And stress often leads to habits like more drinking, smoking, snacking and less exercise.

## Stress and relationships

Stress can make people shorter-tempered, more distant and less affectionate. Couples who are both stressed may find intimacy drops off, which can then become another source of worry. Recognising stress as the common enemy, rather than blaming each other, helps.

## Ways to lower stress

You can't remove all stress, but you can change how your body handles it.

**Calm your body:**

- **Slow breathing** for a few minutes, twice a day. The Breathing program has guided patterns.
- **Move every day.** Exercise is one of the most effective stress relievers there is.
- **Body scans** from the audio library help you notice and release tension.
- **Sleep:** protect 7 to 9 hours as much as you can.

**Calm your mind:**

- **Write it down.** Putting worries on paper, or in your journal, makes them feel more manageable.
- **Separate what you can and can't control,** and focus your energy on the first.
- **Use the thought tools** from the confidence lessons to question catastrophic thinking.
- **Limit news and social media** if they leave you anxious.

**Look after your life:**

- **Talk to someone you trust:** a friend, family member or your partner.
- **Set boundaries** around work time where you can.
- **Do things you enjoy** that have nothing to do with productivity: music, sport, cooking, time outdoors.
- **Cut back on alcohol and caffeine,** which can make anxiety worse.

## A simple daily routine

Try this for two weeks and notice the difference:

- **Morning:** five minutes of daylight and a short walk.
- **Midday:** three minutes of slow breathing.
- **Evening:** write down three things that went well today, and do a body scan or stretch before bed.

## Stress and the pelvic floor

Many people hold stress in their bodies without realising it: a clenched jaw, raised shoulders, a tight belly and a tight pelvic floor. Over time, a constantly tense pelvic floor can contribute to discomfort and can make it harder to relax during intimacy. A quick check a few times a day helps. Notice your jaw, shoulders, belly and pelvic floor, take one slow breath in while letting them soften, and breathe out slowly. The "drops" at the end of each pelvic-floor session train exactly this.

## Small wins count

Stress management isn't about becoming perfectly calm. It's about giving your body regular chances to recover. Even a few minutes of slow breathing, a short walk at lunch or a phone-free evening can lower your stress load. Notice which small habits help you most, and protect them on busy days, when you need them most.

## When stress becomes too much

If you feel constantly overwhelmed, can't switch off, have panic attacks, feel low most days, or have thoughts of harming yourself, please reach out. A doctor, psychologist or counsellor can help. In India, Tele-MANAS (14416) offers free, confidential support any time. In an emergency, call 112.
