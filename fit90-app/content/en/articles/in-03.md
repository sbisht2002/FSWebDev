A common pattern, especially when someone is anxious about performance, is to rush: to move quickly towards intercourse before an erection fades or before ejaculation happens too soon. Rushing usually makes both problems more likely, and it often leaves partners feeling disconnected. Slowing down is one of the simplest and most effective changes you can make.

## Why pace matters

- **Arousal builds over time.** Many people, particularly women, need more time and a wider range of touch to become fully aroused. Moving too fast can leave a partner less ready and less satisfied.
- **Your own control improves.** A slower build-up lets you notice your arousal level and stay in the comfortable zone, as you've practised in the last-longer lessons.
- **Anxiety drops.** When the goal isn't to get to intercourse as fast as possible, there's less to worry about.
- **Connection grows.** Time spent kissing, touching, talking and laughing builds closeness, which makes the whole experience more enjoyable.

## Foreplay isn't a warm-up

It helps to stop thinking of foreplay as the thing that comes before "the main event". For many couples, kissing, cuddling, massage and touching are among the most enjoyable parts of intimacy. Research on couples suggests that variety, including activities other than intercourse, is linked with higher satisfaction for many people.

## Ideas for slowing down

- **Start outside the bedroom:** affection, compliments and attention through the day build connection long before intimacy.
- **Begin with non-sexual touch:** a slow massage, stroking hair, holding hands.
- **Use your senses:** notice warmth, textures, scent and breath. This also keeps you out of "spectating".
- **Take turns:** one person gives touch, the other receives and says what they enjoy, then swap.
- **Breathe together:** slow breathing helps both of you relax and stay present.
- **Pause often:** kiss, talk, laugh. Pauses are part of the experience, not interruptions.

## Pacing and early ejaculation

If you tend to finish quickly, slower pacing combined with breathing and relaxation can give you much more room. It also means your partner's arousal and pleasure aren't tied only to how long intercourse lasts. Many couples find that focusing on each other's enjoyment throughout takes enormous pressure off.

## Pacing and erections

If you worry about losing your erection, slowing down might seem risky. But erections naturally rise and fall during longer intimacy, and that's completely normal. If yours fades, stay relaxed and keep touching and connecting. It will often return, and if not, there are many other ways to enjoy each other.

## A simple slow-down plan

For the next few times you're intimate, try agreeing on a few ground rules together: spend at least the first fifteen minutes on kissing, touching and massage; take at least two pauses to breathe and reconnect; and let either of you say "slower" at any time without needing to explain. Notice how it feels for both of you, and talk about it afterwards. Many couples discover that they enjoy the slower pace much more than they expected.

## Setting the scene

Your surroundings affect how easily you can relax. A comfortable temperature, privacy, soft light, phones on silent and enough time without interruptions all help both of you slow down. It's hard to take things slowly when you're worried about being interrupted or running late.

## Let your partner set the pace sometimes

Asking "What pace feels good for you?" or letting your partner lead can be a relief for both of you. It shares responsibility, builds trust and often reveals what they enjoy most.

## Talk about it

You can raise this outside the bedroom: "I'd love for us to take things slower and enjoy more of the build-up. What do you think?" Most partners welcome the idea.
