Walking is one of the most underrated forms of exercise. It's free, it needs no equipment, it's gentle on your joints, and done briskly it counts as moderate aerobic activity. This plan builds you up over 12 weeks to about 40 minutes, four times a week: the amount linked with better erectile function in research.

## Before you start

- Check with a doctor first if you have chest pain, a heart condition, uncontrolled blood pressure, or you get faint or very breathless with light activity.
- Wear comfortable shoes. Any supportive trainers will do.
- In hot weather, walk early in the morning or in the evening, carry water, and slow down if you feel unwell.
- Stop and seek help if you get chest pain, severe breathlessness, dizziness or pain spreading to your arm or jaw.

## How brisk is brisk?

Aim for a pace where you're breathing harder and feel warm, but can still talk in full sentences. If you can sing, speed up a little. If you can't speak more than a few words, slow down. On a scale of effort from 0 (resting) to 10 (all-out), moderate walking is around 5 or 6.

## The 12-week plan

- **Week 1:** 3 walks of 15 minutes (about 45 minutes in total)
- **Week 2:** 4 walks of 15 minutes (about 60 minutes in total)
- **Week 3:** 4 walks of 20 minutes (about 80 minutes in total)
- **Week 4:** 4 walks of 25 minutes (about 100 minutes in total)
- **Week 5:** 4 walks of 30 minutes (about 120 minutes in total)
- **Week 6:** 4 walks of 30, one of them brisker minutes (about 120 minutes in total)
- **Week 7:** 4 walks of 35 minutes (about 140 minutes in total)
- **Week 8:** 4 walks of 35, include a hill or stairs minutes (about 140 minutes in total)
- **Week 9:** 4 walks of 40 minutes (about 160 minutes in total)
- **Week 10 to 12:** 4 walks of 40, with brisk intervals minutes (about 160 minutes in total)

If a week feels too hard, repeat it. There's no rush. Your 8-week plan in this app includes optional walks that follow a similar build-up, and you can log them from Today or the Exercise program.

## Three difficulty levels

- **Level 1, Walking:** follow the table at a comfortable, brisk pace.
- **Level 2, Brisk with intervals:** within each walk, add 1-minute faster sections every few minutes, like walking as if you're late for a train.
- **Level 3, Walk-jog:** alternate 2 minutes of brisk walking with 1 minute of easy jogging, gradually increasing the jogging time if you enjoy it.

Choose the level that feels challenging but doable. You can move between them.

## Warming up and cooling down

Start each walk at an easy pace for the first three to five minutes, then build to brisk. Finish with a few minutes of easy walking and, if you like, a gentle calf and hip stretch. This helps your heart rate rise and fall gradually and makes the walk feel more comfortable, especially early in the morning.

## Making it stick

- **Anchor it to a habit:** after breakfast, after work, or after dinner.
- **Break it up:** two 20-minute walks count just as well as one 40-minute walk.
- **Make it social:** walk with a friend or partner, or call someone while you walk.
- **Use errands:** walk to the market, take stairs instead of lifts, get off one stop early.
- **Track it:** logging walks in the app keeps you honest and shows your progress.

## Beyond walking

Once walking feels easy, you can swap in any activity you enjoy that gets your heart rate up: cycling, swimming, badminton, football, dancing or skipping. Variety helps you keep going. Add strength training twice a week, using the Fitness 90 home plan, and you'll be meeting the main guidelines for good health.

## What to expect

Most people notice better energy, mood and sleep within two to three weeks. Changes in blood pressure, blood sugar and blood-vessel health build over a few months. In research, the clearest benefits for erections came after about six months of regular exercise, so think of this as a long game that pays off in many ways.
