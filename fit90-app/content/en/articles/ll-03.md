The stop-start technique was described by doctors in the 1950s and is still one of the most widely recommended behavioural approaches for early ejaculation. The idea is simple: you let arousal build, stop before the point of no return, let it settle, and start again. Over time this teaches your body and brain that high arousal doesn't have to mean finishing.

## Before you begin

- Read the lesson on the 0 to 10 arousal scale first. Stop-start relies on it.
- Practise **on your own first**, in private, with no time pressure. This removes performance worries while you learn.
- Plan for about 15 minutes, three or four times a week. Regular practice matters more than long sessions.

## Step by step (solo practice)

1. **Settle.** Lie or sit comfortably. Take two minutes of slow breathing, in for 4 and out for 6.
2. **Build slowly.** Use whatever stimulation you normally would, but go slower than usual. Check your arousal number regularly.
3. **Stop at about 7.** When you notice you're around 7 (or as soon as you notice the early signs of getting close), stop all stimulation completely.
4. **Let it settle.** Breathe slowly, let your belly, buttocks and pelvic floor soften, and wait until your arousal drops to around 4 or 5. This usually takes 30 seconds to a minute or two.
5. **Start again.** Resume stimulation and build back up.
6. **Repeat three or four times,** then allow yourself to finish if you want to, or simply stop.
7. **Log it.** Record the technique ("stop-start") and your sense of control from 0 to 10.

## Making it harder, gradually

As control improves over a few weeks:

- Stop at a higher number, such as 8 instead of 7.
- Shorten the pauses.
- Use stimulation that feels more intense.
- Replace full stops with slowing down, combined with slow breathing and relaxing the pelvic floor.

The goal is to need fewer and shorter pauses while still feeling in control.

## A sample first two weeks

- **Week 1:** three sessions, stopping at around 6 or 7, with long, unhurried pauses. Focus on noticing, not on lasting longer.
- **Week 2:** three or four sessions, stopping at 7 to 8, with slightly shorter pauses. Add slow breathing during every pause.

If a session goes badly, simply note it and try again another day. There's no exam to pass.

## Common pitfalls

**Stopping too late.** If you pass the point of no return, that's okay. Note what you felt just before and stop earlier next time.

**Tensing up during the pause.** Gripping your muscles to hold back tends to backfire. Use the pause to relax, not to clench.

**Rushing through.** The slow build is part of the training. Going fast teaches your body to go fast.

**Practising only when anxious.** Practise when you're relaxed and have time, so your brain links high arousal with calm.

## What the evidence says

A Cochrane review of behavioural therapies for early ejaculation found some evidence that they help, particularly when combined with medicines, though the studies were few and of limited quality. Guidelines suggest behavioural approaches are best for men who are motivated to practise, and that combining them with medical treatment often works better than either alone. Benefits can fade if you stop practising completely, so it's worth keeping up occasionally once you've built control.

## Bringing it to a partner

Once you feel reasonably confident on your own, you can practise with a partner, who can help with the stopping and starting. Read the lesson on moving from solo to partnered practice for how to talk about this and keep it relaxed and enjoyable for both of you.
