If you feel you finish sooner than you'd like, you're in very large company. Surveys suggest that around one in five men worry about it at some point. Early ejaculation, often called premature ejaculation or PE, is one of the most common sexual concerns men have, and one of the most treatable.

## What actually counts as early?

Films and porn give a very distorted picture. In real life, studies that timed couples with a stopwatch found that the typical time from penetration to ejaculation was around five to six minutes, with a wide normal range. Plenty of men and their partners are happy with less than that.

Doctors usually talk about early ejaculation when three things are true together:

- It happens **quickly**, often within about a minute of penetration (or even before) for lifelong cases, or noticeably sooner than before for acquired cases.
- You **can't delay it** on all or nearly all occasions.
- It causes **distress**, frustration or avoidance of intimacy for you or your partner.

The distress part matters. If you and your partner are both happy, there's no problem to fix.

## Lifelong or acquired?

**Lifelong** early ejaculation has been there since your first sexual experiences. It's thought to relate partly to how your nervous system and brain chemistry are set up, so it isn't your fault and it isn't about willpower. In European urology guidelines, **medical treatment is the first-line option** for lifelong early ejaculation. Behavioural techniques like the ones in this app can be combined with it.

**Acquired** early ejaculation developed later, after a time when things were different. Common contributors include anxiety, relationship stress, erection difficulties (rushing because you're worried about losing the erection), thyroid problems and prostate inflammation. Finding and dealing with the cause often helps a lot.

There are also "variable" patterns, where it happens sometimes but not always. That's very normal and usually not a medical problem.

## What helps

There are several approaches, and they often work best together:

- **Behavioural techniques:** arousal awareness, the stop-start method and the squeeze technique. A Cochrane review found that behavioural therapy can help, especially combined with medication, while noting the evidence was limited.
- **Pelvic-floor training:** small studies suggest it may help some men gain control. The evidence is promising but low to moderate in quality.
- **Breathing and relaxation:** tension tends to speed things up. Learning to stay relaxed while aroused is a core skill.
- **Medical treatment:** a doctor can discuss options such as specific tablets or numbing creams or sprays. These are effective for many men and are the first-line treatment for lifelong cases.
- **Working on anxiety and communication:** worry about finishing early can make it more likely, and talking openly with a partner usually takes the pressure down.

## What doesn't help

- **Distraction tricks** like thinking about work. They can take you out of the moment and reduce enjoyment for both of you, and they don't build lasting control.
- **Unregulated "delay" supplements** sold online. Some have been found to contain hidden prescription drugs. This app never recommends supplements.
- **Shame.** It makes anxiety worse, and anxiety makes early ejaculation worse.

## How this program works

Over the next lessons you'll learn to rate your arousal on a 0 to 10 scale, notice the point of no return, practise stop-start and squeeze techniques in private first, use breathing and pelvic-floor relaxation during arousal, and then, if you have a partner, bring the skills into time together. You'll log only the technique and a 0 to 10 sense of control, nothing graphic.

Be patient. Most men need several weeks of regular practice. And if your early ejaculation has always been there and bothers you a lot, please talk to a doctor too.
