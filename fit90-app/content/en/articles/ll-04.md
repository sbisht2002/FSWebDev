The squeeze technique is a close cousin of stop-start. It was developed by the researchers Masters and Johnson in the 1970s. Instead of simply pausing, you apply gentle pressure to briefly reduce the urge to ejaculate, then continue. Some men prefer it to stop-start; others find stop-start simpler. It's worth trying both and keeping what works for you.

## How it works

Firm pressure just below the head of the penis, for a few seconds, can temporarily reduce the urge to ejaculate and may slightly reduce the erection. Arousal then settles a little, and you can carry on. Repeated over weeks, this helps you learn to stay at high arousal for longer without tipping over.

## Before you start

- Make sure you're comfortable with the 0 to 10 arousal scale.
- Practise **on your own first**, in private, with plenty of time.
- Trim your nails and be gentle. The pressure should be firm but never painful.

## Step by step (solo practice)

1. **Settle** with two minutes of slow breathing.
2. **Build arousal slowly,** checking your number as you go.
3. **At about 7,** or as soon as you notice the early signs of getting close, stop stimulation.
4. **Squeeze:** place your thumb on the underside of the penis just below the head, with your first and second fingers on the top side. Squeeze firmly for about 10 to 20 seconds.
5. **Release and rest** for 30 seconds or so. Breathe slowly and let your belly and pelvic floor relax. The urge should fade.
6. **Start again** and repeat three or four times before finishing, or stop if you prefer.
7. **Log it** in the practice log: the technique and your 0 to 10 sense of control.

If you prefer, you can squeeze at the base of the penis instead. Some people find this easier, particularly later with a partner.

## Getting it right

- **Too early is better than too late.** If you pass the point of no return, the squeeze won't stop it. That's fine. Next time, start earlier.
- **Firm, not painful.** If it hurts, ease off. Pain is a signal to stop.
- **Pair it with breathing.** The squeeze works best when you also slow your breathing and soften your pelvic floor during the rest.
- **Be patient.** Like any skill, it takes regular practice over several weeks. Three short sessions a week is a good rhythm to aim for.

## Squeeze or stop-start?

Both are based on the same principle: approach high arousal, step back, repeat. Stop-start is simpler and doesn't need any extra action. The squeeze can feel more effective for some men because it gives a quicker reset. Guidelines group both together as behavioural therapy, and there isn't good evidence that one is better than the other. Choose the one you're more likely to keep doing.

## With a partner

Later, a partner can help apply the squeeze, which can turn practice into something you do together rather than something you hide. The lesson on moving to partnered practice covers how to bring this up and keep it low-pressure. Clear, kind communication matters more than perfect technique.

## A realistic view

Behavioural techniques help many men, especially when they practise regularly, and they combine well with medical treatment. They don't work for everyone, and benefits can fade if practice stops completely. Progress is rarely a straight line either: good weeks and harder weeks are both normal, especially when you're tired or stressed. If your early ejaculation is lifelong and distressing, please talk to a doctor as well, as medicines are the first-line option in European guidelines.
