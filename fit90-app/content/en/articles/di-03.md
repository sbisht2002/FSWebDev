Flavonoids are natural compounds found in many fruits, vegetables, tea and cocoa. They give many plants their colours. Research suggests they may support healthy blood vessels, which matter for erections and for your heart.

## What the research found

A large study from the United States followed more than 25,000 men for about ten years. Men who ate the most flavonoid-rich foods, particularly those containing compounds called anthocyanins, flavanones and flavones, were less likely to develop erection problems. The link was strongest in men under 70. The foods that contributed most were berries, citrus fruits, apples, pears and red wine.

The researchers also found that men who ate more of these foods **and** were physically active had the lowest risk of all. That fits with the bigger picture: diet and exercise together have the strongest effect.

It's important to be honest about what this shows. This kind of study tracks what people eat and what happens to them. It can't prove that flavonoids cause the benefit, because people who eat more fruit may also have other healthy habits. But the findings fit with what we know about how flavonoids act on blood vessels, and these foods are good for you in many other ways too.

## Flavonoid-rich foods available in India

- **Berries and dark fruits:** jamun, black grapes, strawberries in season, blueberries (if affordable), pomegranate
- **Citrus:** oranges, mosambi, kinnow, lemon, amla
- **Apples, pears, guava**
- **Onions** (especially red onions), **tomatoes, capsicum**
- **Leafy greens:** palak, methi, coriander, mint
- **Tea:** green tea and regular black tea both contain flavonoids. Go easy on the sugar.
- **Cocoa:** a small piece of dark chocolate with a high cocoa content, as an occasional treat
- **Herbs and spices:** many common spices contain helpful plant compounds

## What about red wine?

Red wine appeared in the study, but this is not a reason to start drinking. Alcohol carries real risks, including for erections, and any benefit from wine's flavonoids is easily outweighed by alcohol's harms. You can get the same flavonoids from black grapes, jamun and berries without the alcohol.

## Easy ways to eat more colour

- Add a fruit to breakfast: a sliced orange, a handful of grapes or a guava.
- Squeeze lemon over dal, salad or chana.
- Keep a bowl of seasonal fruit where you can see it.
- Add chopped onion, tomato and coriander to sprouts, poha or eggs.
- Swap one sugary drink a day for green tea or lemon water.
- Aim for at least two different colours on your plate at lunch and dinner.

## A week of colour, without extra cost

Seasonal fruit is usually the cheapest and freshest. In summer, think jamun, mango in moderation and watermelon; in winter, oranges, kinnow, guava and amla. Buying from a local market at the end of the day can be cheaper still. Frozen peas, spinach and berries are also good, affordable options and keep their nutrients well.

Try a simple rule: one fruit with breakfast, one colourful vegetable with lunch, and one with dinner. That's three servings a day without much effort, and you'll naturally take in a wide range of plant compounds, fibre and vitamins.

## Whole foods, not pills

Flavonoid supplements and "enhancement" pills are not a substitute for food. Supplements aren't tested the same way as medicines, and some products sold for sexual performance have been found to contain hidden prescription drugs. This app never recommends supplements. Real fruits and vegetables also give you fibre, vitamins and minerals that pills can't.

If you have diabetes, heart disease or kidney disease, please talk to your doctor or a registered dietitian before making big changes to your diet, as your needs may be different. This app never recommends supplements.
