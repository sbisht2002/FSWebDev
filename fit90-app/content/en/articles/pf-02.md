Pelvic-floor exercises look simple, but studies in both men and women show that many people do them wrongly at first. The good news is that the common mistakes are easy to spot and easy to fix. A few careful squeezes done well are worth far more than lots done badly.

## Mistake 1: Holding your breath

When you concentrate on squeezing, it's natural to stop breathing. Holding your breath raises the pressure inside your belly and pushes down on the very muscles you're trying to lift.

**Fix it:** breathe normally through every squeeze. Some people find it helps to breathe out gently as they squeeze and lift, and breathe in as they let go. You can also count out loud quietly, because you can't hold your breath while you're talking.

## Mistake 2: Squeezing your buttocks

Your buttock muscles are big and strong, so they love to join in. If your bottom lifts or tightens, or your hips move, the buttocks are doing the work.

**Fix it:** lie on your back with your knees bent and let your knees fall slightly apart. Place a hand on one buttock. Squeeze your pelvic floor and check that the buttock stays soft. Make the squeeze smaller if needed.

## Mistake 3: Pulling in your belly hard

A little gentle tightening low down in the belly is normal, because the deep belly muscles work together with the pelvic floor. But if your whole stomach sucks in, or your ribs move, you're using the wrong muscles.

**Fix it:** rest a hand on your belly just above the pubic bone. Aim for a light lift underneath, with the hand barely moving.

## Mistake 4: Squeezing your thighs together

If your knees move towards each other, your inner thigh muscles are doing the work.

**Fix it:** put a cushion between your knees and keep it loose, or let your knees fall gently apart.

## Mistake 5: Pushing down instead of lifting

Some people bear down, as if straining on the toilet, instead of lifting up. This is the opposite of what you want.

**Fix it:** think "up and in", like drawing the base of the penis towards your belly button, or stopping wind. If you used the mirror check from the first lesson, the base of the penis should draw in and the testicles lift slightly, not push down.

## Mistake 6: Not letting go fully

This one matters more than most people realise. If you never fully relax between squeezes, the muscle stays tense, gets tired quickly and can become sore. Over time, a pelvic floor that can't relax may contribute to pain or difficulty with ejaculation.

**Fix it:** give the release as much attention as the squeeze. In this app, the rest after each squeeze is at least as long as the hold. Feel the muscle soften and drop, and wait for it to settle before the next squeeze.

## Mistake 7: Doing too much, too soon

More isn't better. Long sessions of hard squeezing can leave your pelvic floor tired and achy, and tired muscles squeeze badly.

**Fix it:** follow the levels. Move up only after several sessions that felt fairly easy. If a session felt very hard, it's fine to go back a level for a while. If anything hurts, stop.

## Mistake 8: Doing them while urinating

Stopping your urine flow once can help you find the muscles. Doing it regularly can stop your bladder from emptying properly and may raise the risk of urine infections.

**Fix it:** do your exercises with an empty bladder, not on the toilet.

## A quick checklist

Before each squeeze, check in:

- Am I breathing?
- Are my buttocks, thighs and belly soft?
- Am I lifting up and in, not pushing down?
- Did I let go completely last time?

If you're unsure whether you're doing it right after a few weeks, a pelvic-floor physiotherapist can check your technique. In studies, many men were taught by a physiotherapist, and good teaching may be part of why the exercises helped.
