Your pelvic floor is a group of muscles that sits like a hammock at the bottom of your pelvis, running from your pubic bone at the front to your tailbone at the back. You use these muscles all day without thinking about them. They help hold in urine and wind, support your bladder and bowel, and play a part in sexual function.

Two of these muscles matter most for sexual health. One wraps around the base of the penis and helps keep blood in it during an erection. The other helps push semen out during ejaculation. Training them is often called doing "Kegels", after the doctor who first described the exercises.

## Why bother training them?

Small studies suggest that pelvic-floor training can help some men with erection problems and with early ejaculation. A systematic review in 2019 found that training seemed to help both, while noting that the studies were small and of low to moderate quality, and that no single best routine has been proven. Many of the men in these studies were taught by a physiotherapist, which may matter. So it is fair to say pelvic-floor training is promising, low-risk and worth trying, but it is not a magic switch.

The other half of the skill is **letting go**. A pelvic floor that is always tight can cause its own problems, including pain and difficulty relaxing. That is why every session in this app ends with "drops", where you practise releasing.

## How to find the right muscles

Try one of these, and use whichever works for you:

1. **The "hold in wind" method.** Imagine you are in a quiet room and need to stop yourself passing wind. Gently tighten the muscle you'd use. You should feel a lift and squeeze around the back passage.
2. **The "stop the flow" check.** Once, and only once, try to slow or stop your urine flow midway. The muscle that does that is your pelvic floor. Don't make this a habit, because regularly stopping the flow can stop your bladder emptying properly.
3. **The mirror check.** Standing in front of a mirror without clothes, tighten the muscles. If you've found the right ones, the base of the penis draws in slightly and the testicles lift a little.

You can also lie down and place your fingertips just behind your testicles, on the area called the perineum. When you squeeze correctly, you'll feel it tighten and lift away from your fingers.

## What it should feel like

A correct squeeze feels like a gentle **lift and squeeze** inwards and upwards, as if drawing the base of the penis up towards your belly button. It is a small movement. Nobody watching you should be able to tell you are doing it.

Your buttocks, thighs and belly should stay soft. You should keep breathing normally the whole time. If you notice yourself holding your breath, clenching your bottom or pulling your stomach in hard, ease off and try a gentler squeeze.

## Your first practice

Lie on your back with your knees bent and feet flat. This is the easiest position because your muscles aren't working against gravity.

- Breathe in and out slowly a few times.
- Gently squeeze and lift for about three seconds.
- Let go completely for three seconds. Feel the muscle drop back down.
- Repeat a few times, then rest.

That's it. Level 1 of the trainer is built around exactly this. Quality matters much more than quantity, so a few careful squeezes beat lots of sloppy ones.

## When to stop

Pelvic-floor exercises should never hurt. Stop and see a doctor or a pelvic-floor physiotherapist if you get pain in the pelvis, testicles or penis, pain when urinating, or new urinary problems. If you have pelvic pain, the app will give you a relaxation-first version that focuses on letting go.
