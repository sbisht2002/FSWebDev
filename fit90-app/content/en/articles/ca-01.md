Performance anxiety is the worry that you won't "perform" sexually: that you won't get or keep an erection, that you'll finish too soon, or that you won't satisfy your partner. It's extremely common, especially in younger men and at the start of new relationships. It's also one of the most understandable things a body can do.

## Why worry affects erections and control

Arousal and erections rely on your body's relaxed mode. Blood vessels in the penis need to open up, and that happens most easily when you feel safe and engaged. Anxiety switches on the opposite, "fight or flight" mode. Stress hormones tighten blood vessels, attention narrows onto threat, and the body prepares for action rather than pleasure.

So when you're worried about losing your erection, the worry itself can make it harder to keep. When you're anxious about finishing quickly, the tension and rushing can make it happen sooner. Neither of these means anything is wrong with you. Your body is responding exactly as bodies do to stress.

## The worry loop

Performance anxiety often grows through a loop:

1. Something doesn't go as hoped one time, perhaps because you were tired, had a drink or were nervous.
2. Next time, you worry it'll happen again.
3. The worry triggers anxiety, which makes the problem more likely.
4. It happens again, and the belief "I can't do this" gets stronger.
5. You start to dread or avoid intimacy, which adds even more pressure.

The good news is that loops can be broken at several points: by changing how you think about it, by shifting your attention, by relaxing your body, and by taking the pressure off.

## Spectating

The sex researchers Masters and Johnson noticed that anxious men often mentally step outside themselves and watch, checking: "Is it working? Am I hard enough? How long has it been?" They called this **spectatoring**.

Spectating pulls your attention away from sensation and towards judgement. Without sensation, arousal fades. It's one of the main ways anxiety interferes, and it can be trained out with practice. You'll learn how in the lesson on moving from spectating to sensation.

## What helps

There's no single proven treatment for performance anxiety, but several approaches help many men:

- **Understanding it,** which you're doing now. Knowing that it's a normal stress response reduces fear.
- **Working with thoughts:** spotting anxious predictions and answering them more realistically (CBT-style skills).
- **Attention training:** coming back to sensation instead of spectating.
- **Calming the body:** slow breathing and pelvic-floor relaxation before and during intimacy.
- **Small steps:** gradually approaching what you fear rather than avoiding it.
- **Taking the pressure off:** for example through sensate focus with a partner, where intercourse is set aside for a while.
- **Self-compassion:** treating yourself as you'd treat a friend in the same situation.
- **Support:** a psychologist or qualified sex therapist if anxiety is strong or persistent.

## Is it "just anxiety"?

Sometimes erection problems are caused by anxiety alone. Sometimes there's a physical cause too, and anxiety piles on top. A useful clue is whether you get erections on waking or when alone. If you never do, or if problems came on suddenly, or you have health conditions like diabetes or high blood pressure, please see a doctor. Treating a physical cause often eases the anxiety as well.
