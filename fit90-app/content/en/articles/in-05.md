A lot of sexual worry comes from comparing ourselves with an imaginary standard: what we've seen in porn and films, heard from friends, or picked up as "what real men do". These standards are usually unrealistic, and measuring yourself against them is a fast route to anxiety. Knowing what's actually normal can be a huge relief.

## Common myths, and what's real

**Myth: "Sex should last a long time."**
Real-life studies that timed couples found the typical length of intercourse to be around five to six minutes, with a wide range. Longer isn't automatically better. Many partners enjoy the whole experience, including touch and closeness, far more than the length of intercourse.

**Myth: "A man should always be ready."**
Desire and erections are affected by tiredness, stress, alcohol, mood, health and how connected you feel. Not being in the mood, or not getting an erection every time, is a normal part of being human.

**Myth: "Erections should be instant and last throughout."**
Erections naturally rise and fall during longer intimacy, especially with distractions or nerves. A fading erection often returns with relaxation and touch.

**Myth: "Both partners should orgasm every time, ideally together."**
This is rare in reality, and treating it as the goal adds pressure. Many people have satisfying intimacy without orgasm every time, and simultaneous orgasm is the exception rather than the rule.

**Myth: "More sex means a happier relationship."**
Research suggests that well-being rises with sexual frequency up to around once a week on average, after which there's no further clear benefit. Quality and connection matter more than quantity, and every couple's rhythm is different.

**Myth: "Size and stamina are what partners care about most."**
When people describe great intimacy, they talk about feeling desired, respected, relaxed and connected, and about a partner who is attentive. Physical "specs" come far down the list.

**Myth: "Everyone else is having amazing sex all the time."**
People rarely talk honestly about difficulties, so it's easy to believe everyone else finds it easy. In reality, concerns about erections, timing and desire are extremely common.

## Where unrealistic standards come from

- **Porn:** edited, scripted and performed, often with breaks, retakes and sometimes medication.
- **Films and TV:** designed to be dramatic, not accurate.
- **Friends and social media:** people tend to exaggerate and leave out the awkward parts.
- **Advertising:** selling products by creating insecurity.

## Healthier expectations

- Intimacy varies from day to day, and that's normal.
- Some experiences will be wonderful, some ordinary, and some disappointing. All of that is part of a healthy sex life.
- Communication, curiosity and kindness matter more than technique.
- Every couple is different. What matters is what works for the two of you.

## Bodies change, and that's normal

Erections, desire and stamina naturally change with age, health, stress and life stages such as a new job, a new baby or caring for family. A body that responds differently at 40 than at 20 isn't broken. Adjusting expectations to fit your real life, rather than an imagined ideal, makes it much easier to enjoy intimacy at every stage.

## Talking about expectations

It can help to talk with your partner about what each of you actually wants. You may find that their expectations are much more relaxed than the ones you've been putting on yourself. That conversation alone can remove a lot of pressure.

## When expectations cause real distress

If unrealistic standards are causing a lot of anxiety, or affecting your relationship, a qualified sex therapist or psychologist can help you work through them. The confidence lessons in this app are another good place to start.
