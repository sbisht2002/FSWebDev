Control starts with noticing. Many men who finish sooner than they'd like describe going from "fine" to "too late" with nothing in between. In reality there's almost always a build-up. The skill is learning to notice it early enough to do something about it. That's what arousal awareness is.

## The 0 to 10 scale

Think of your arousal as a number:

- **0:** not aroused at all
- **2 to 3:** early interest, relaxed
- **4 to 5:** clearly aroused, comfortable, in control
- **6 to 7:** high arousal, still comfortable, still choosing
- **8:** getting close, this is the time to slow down or pause
- **9:** very close, it's hard to hold back
- **10:** the point of no return, ejaculation is going to happen

There's no right number to be at. The goal is simply to know where you are. Most men find that if they ease off around 7 or 8, they can come back down and keep going. Waiting until 9 is usually too late.

## Why noticing is hard at first

When you're anxious, your attention goes to worry ("Is this going to happen again?") rather than to your body. When you're distracted, you miss the early signs. And if you've always rushed, your brain simply hasn't had practice reading the signals. All of this can be learned.

## Body signals to watch for

Everyone is different, but common signs that arousal is climbing include:

- faster, shallower breathing
- tightening in the belly, thighs, buttocks or pelvic floor
- a rising feeling of urgency or "rushing"
- warmth and changes in sensation in the pelvic area
- a feeling of pressure building low in the pelvis just before the point of no return

As you practise, you'll get better at telling a 6 from an 8.

## How to practise

Start in private, on your own, when you're relaxed and have time. This lets you focus completely on your body without any pressure.

1. Begin with two or three minutes of slow breathing (in 4, out 6) to settle.
2. As arousal builds, check in every so often and give yourself a number.
3. Notice what changes as the number rises: your breathing, muscle tension, thoughts.
4. When you reach about 6 or 7, slow everything down, breathe slowly and let your pelvic floor relax. Watch the number drift down to 4 or 5.
5. Repeat the rise and fall a few times before finishing.

Afterwards, log a practice in the app: just the technique ("awareness") and how much control you felt from 0 to 10. Nothing else is recorded.

## Using the scale with breathing

Breathing is your main dial. Quick, shallow breathing tends to push arousal up. Slow belly breathing with a long breath out tends to bring it down. A gentle "drop" of the pelvic floor, as practised in your trainer sessions, helps too. Tension is often what tips arousal over the edge, so softening it gives you more room.

## Using the scale in everyday life

You can practise noticing without anything sexual at all. Rate your stress from 0 to 10 a few times a day, or notice how your breathing changes when you're rushed. The habit of checking in with your body, naming a number and then choosing to slow down is the same skill, and everyday practice makes it easier to use when it matters.

## Be kind to yourself

You'll overshoot sometimes, especially early on. That's useful information, not failure. Notice what the signs were just before, and you'll catch them sooner next time. Most men see steady improvement after a few weeks of regular practice.

## Next steps
