Many common medicines can affect desire, erections or ejaculation. This is a normal topic for doctors, and there are often good solutions. The most important rule: **never stop or change a prescribed medicine on your own.** Some medicines are dangerous to stop suddenly, and the condition they treat may be more serious than the side effect.

## Medicines that can affect sexual function

Not everyone gets side effects, and the list below isn't complete. Common groups include:

- **Some blood pressure medicines,** such as certain diuretics and beta-blockers. Untreated high blood pressure itself also harms erections, so treatment is still very important.
- **Antidepressants,** especially the type called SSRIs. They can reduce desire, delay ejaculation or make orgasm harder to reach. Depression itself also affects sexual function.
- **Some anti-anxiety medicines and sleeping tablets.**
- **Antipsychotic medicines,** some of which affect hormone levels.
- **Some prostate medicines and hair-loss medicines,** which can affect ejaculation or desire in some men.
- **Opioid painkillers,** used long term, which can lower testosterone.
- **Some anti-epilepsy medicines.**
- **Antihistamines and some stomach medicines** occasionally.
- **Anabolic steroids** used for bodybuilding, which can shut down your own testosterone production and cause lasting problems.

Recreational drugs and alcohol can also have strong effects.

## Working out whether a medicine is the cause

Clues that a medicine may be involved:

- the problem started soon after beginning a new medicine or changing a dose
- the leaflet lists sexual side effects
- you don't have other obvious causes

Sometimes it's hard to tell whether the medicine or the underlying condition is responsible. A doctor can help untangle this.

## What a doctor might suggest

Depending on your situation, options can include:

- waiting a few weeks, as some side effects ease with time
- changing the dose or the time of day you take it
- switching to a different medicine in the same family with fewer sexual side effects
- adding a treatment for the side effect, such as a medicine for erections, where safe
- treating the underlying condition differently

## Keeping a simple record

If you're not sure whether a medicine is affecting you, keep a short note for a few weeks: when you started or changed it, and any changes in desire, erections or ejaculation. Your weekly check-in in this app can help. Bring the notes to your appointment. Clear information helps your doctor decide whether to adjust anything.

## Don't forget the condition itself

Many conditions treated by these medicines, such as high blood pressure, depression, anxiety and diabetes, can affect sexual function on their own. Sometimes treating the condition well actually improves sexual health, even if the medicine has some side effects. That's another reason to make any changes together with your doctor rather than alone.

## Supplements and over-the-counter products

Herbal products, bodybuilding supplements and medicines bought without a prescription can also affect hormones and sexual function, or interact with prescribed medicines. Always mention them to your doctor or pharmacist, even if they're labelled "natural".

## How to bring it up

It's normal to feel awkward, but doctors are used to this conversation. You could say: "Since starting this tablet, I've noticed changes in my sex life. Could that be related, and are there alternatives?" Bring a list of all your medicines, including supplements, herbal products and anything bought online.

## Important safety notes

- **Never combine medicines for erections with nitrates** (often prescribed for chest pain as tablets or sprays). The combination can cause a dangerous drop in blood pressure.
- **Avoid buying "enhancement" products online.** Some contain hidden prescription drugs that can interact with your medicines.
- **An erection lasting more than four hours is an emergency.** Call 112 or go to the nearest emergency department.
