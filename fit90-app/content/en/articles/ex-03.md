Aerobic exercise does most of the heavy lifting for erection health, but strength training, core work and mobility round things out. They help with body composition, blood-sugar control, posture, back comfort, confidence, and they give you a natural place to practise pelvic-floor control.

## Strength training twice a week

Guidelines recommend muscle-strengthening activities on at least two days a week, working all the major muscle groups. You can do this at home with no equipment or at a gym.

**Home version (no equipment):** the Fitness 90 program in this app is a complete 90-day calisthenics plan with three levels, animated demonstrations and automatic progression. It covers push, pull and legs with core work. If you want something simpler, this twice-weekly circuit works:

- Squats or sit-to-stand from a chair: 3 sets of 10 to 15
- Push-ups (on the wall, a table or the floor): 3 sets of 8 to 12
- Glute bridges: 3 sets of 12 to 15
- Reverse lunges: 3 sets of 8 to 10 each leg
- Plank: 3 holds of 20 to 45 seconds
- Rows with a backpack or water bottles: 3 sets of 10 to 12

**Gym version:** goblet squats, leg press, dumbbell bench or chest press, seated cable rows or lat pulldowns, Romanian deadlifts, and a plank or cable core exercise. Choose a weight you can lift for 8 to 12 controlled repetitions with good form, and add a little weight when it gets easy.

## Three difficulty levels

- **Level 1:** 2 sets of each exercise, easier versions (wall push-ups, chair squats).
- **Level 2:** 3 sets, standard versions.
- **Level 3:** 3 to 4 sets, harder versions (decline push-ups, split squats, longer planks) or heavier weights.

Move up when the last set feels comfortably achievable for two sessions in a row.

## Bringing in the pelvic floor

Many strength exercises are a natural fit for gentle pelvic-floor work:

- **Glute bridge:** squeeze and lift the pelvic floor as your hips rise, release as you lower.
- **Squat:** breathe out and gently lift the pelvic floor as you stand up.
- **Lifting anything heavy:** a light pelvic-floor lift on the effort can support your core.

Keep these squeezes gentle and always let go fully afterwards. Don't hold your breath. If you have pelvic pain, skip the squeezes and focus on relaxation instead.

## Core and hip mobility

Tight hips and a stiff lower back are common in people who sit a lot, and tension in this area can go hand in hand with a tense pelvic floor. A few minutes of mobility helps:

- **Cat-cow:** on hands and knees, slowly round and then arch your back, 8 to 10 times.
- **Hip-flexor stretch:** in a half-kneeling position, gently push the hips forward, 30 seconds each side.
- **Butterfly stretch:** sitting with the soles of your feet together, let your knees drop, breathing slowly, 45 seconds.
- **Child's pose:** kneel, sit back on your heels and reach your arms forward, breathing into your lower back, 45 seconds.
- **Deep squat hold:** holding onto something if needed, 30 to 45 seconds.

## Yoga-style stretching

A short stretching routine two or three times a week, especially in the evening, supports relaxation and sleep. Combine slow stretching with the breathing patterns from the Breathing program: breathe in as you prepare, breathe out as you ease into the stretch.

## Safety

Start lighter than you think you need to and focus on form. Mild muscle soreness for a day or two is normal; sharp pain is not. If you have heart disease, uncontrolled blood pressure, chest pain on exertion, or a joint or back problem, check with a doctor or physiotherapist before starting.
