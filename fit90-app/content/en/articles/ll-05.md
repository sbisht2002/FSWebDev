Here's something that surprises many men: trying hard to hold back often makes things happen faster. When arousal climbs and you grit your teeth, clench your buttocks and hold your breath, your body reads that as a high-alert state, and high alert tends to speed ejaculation up. Staying loose and breathing slowly gives you more room.

This lesson brings together three skills you've already met: slow breathing, the arousal scale and the pelvic-floor "drop".

## What tension does

Ejaculation is partly a reflex. Tension in the belly, buttocks, thighs and pelvic floor, along with fast breathing, all feed into that reflex. Many men also tense up because they're anxious about finishing early, which creates a loop: worry leads to tension, tension speeds things up, and that confirms the worry.

Learning to relax while aroused breaks the loop. It's not about feeling less. Most men find they enjoy sensations more when they're relaxed.

## The three dials

**1. Your breath.** Slow, low breathing with a long breath out is the most powerful dial you have. Breathe in through your nose for about four seconds, letting your belly rise, then breathe out for about six seconds.

**2. Your pelvic floor.** When you notice arousal climbing past 6 or 7, check your pelvic floor. If it's gripping, let it soften and drop on the in-breath, just as you practise at the end of each trainer session. This is the opposite of the squeeze you might expect.

**3. Your pace.** Slowing down movement or stimulation gives your breath and muscles time to settle.

## A practice routine (solo)

1. Start with three minutes of slow breathing.
2. Let arousal build gradually, rating it from 0 to 10 every so often.
3. At around 6, do a body scan: jaw, shoulders, belly, buttocks, thighs, pelvic floor. Soften anything that's tight.
4. Keep breathing slowly and continue. If you rise to 7 or 8, slow down and take three long breaths out, letting the pelvic floor drop.
5. Notice whether your number levels off or drifts down. Continue when it does.
6. Repeat for 10 to 15 minutes, then log your practice with the technique "breathing".

Over a few weeks, aim to need fewer pauses, using breathing and relaxation instead of stopping completely.

## Squeeze or relax: which is it?

You may have read that squeezing the pelvic floor can delay ejaculation. Some men do find a firm squeeze at the right moment helps, and pelvic-floor training studies suggest stronger, better-controlled muscles may help with control overall. But squeezing at high arousal can also tip some men over the edge. The most useful skill is **control**: being able to squeeze and to let go on demand. That's why your trainer builds both. Experiment in solo practice to learn what works for your body.

## Adding it to the other techniques

Breathing and relaxation fit into stop-start and squeeze practice too. Every pause becomes a chance to breathe slowly and release tension. Many men find that this, more than the pauses themselves, is what builds lasting control.

## When anxiety is high

If anxious thoughts keep pulling you out of the moment, try the confidence lessons, especially the one on shifting attention from "spectating" to sensation. A short calm-down routine before intimacy, from the audio library, can lower your starting point so you have more room.

## Be patient with yourself

Relaxing on purpose while aroused is a new skill for most men. It can feel strange at first. It usually takes a few weeks of regular practice before it starts to feel natural, and some days will go better than others. That's normal.
