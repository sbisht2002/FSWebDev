Sensate focus is a set of structured touching exercises for couples, developed by Masters and Johnson in the 1970s and still widely used by sex therapists today. The core idea is simple: for a while, you take intercourse and orgasm off the table and focus on touch and sensation. With the pressure gone, anxiety drops, attention returns to the body, and closeness grows.

It's used for erection difficulties, early ejaculation, performance anxiety and low desire, and many couples simply enjoy it. Reviews of the research describe it as widely used and well regarded, though high-quality trials are limited.

## Ground rules for every stage

- **No intercourse and no orgasm** during the exercises, until you both agree to move on. This is the most important rule. It removes the goal, and with it the fear of failing.
- **Take turns** being the "toucher" and the "receiver". Sessions are usually 15 to 30 minutes each way.
- **Touch for your own interest.** The toucher explores what textures, shapes and temperatures feel like to **them**, rather than trying to arouse their partner.
- **Notice sensation.** When the mind wanders to judging or planning, gently return to what you can feel.
- **Speak up.** The receiver says if anything is uncomfortable. Either of you can pause or stop at any time.
- **Choose a calm time,** with privacy and no rush. Two or three sessions a week works well.

## Stage 1: Non-genital touch

Take turns touching each other's body, avoiding the chest, breasts and genitals. Use hands only at first. Explore the back, arms, legs, face, hands and feet. Notice temperature, texture and pressure.

**Talk about it afterwards:** "What did you notice? What felt pleasant? Was anything uncomfortable?"

Stay at this stage until it feels relaxed and enjoyable, usually a week or two.

## Stage 2: Whole-body touch

Now the chest and genitals can be included, but touching them should be part of exploring the whole body, not the main event. The aim is still curiosity and sensation, not arousal. If arousal happens, simply notice it. If it fades, notice that too. Neither is a problem.

**Conversation prompt:** "What surprised you this time?"

## Stage 3: Guiding each other

The receiver places a hand gently over the toucher's hand to show pressure, speed and places they enjoy. This builds the habit of communicating without words, and it's a lovely way to learn about each other.

**Conversation prompt:** "Was there anything you'd like more of, or less of?"

## Stage 4: Mutual touch

Instead of taking turns, you touch each other at the same time. Keep the focus on sensation rather than goals. For couples working on early ejaculation, this is a good point to bring in the stop-start or squeeze technique with your partner's help.

## Moving on

When you both feel relaxed and confident, you can gradually bring intercourse back, still with the same attitude: slow, curious and with permission to pause or stop. Many couples keep using sensate-focus sessions now and then, simply because they enjoy them.

## Practical tips

- **Set the scene:** a warm room, phones away, soft light. Comfort helps the body relax.
- **Agree on a stop word** either of you can use, with no need to explain.
- **Use lotion or oil** if you both like it, to change the textures you notice.
- **Keep it light:** laughter is welcome. This isn't a test.
- **Log it** in the app as a sensate-focus session if it's part of your plan.

## When it feels difficult

It's common for old worries to come up, especially when you reach a stage that previously caused anxiety. That's useful information. Go back to the previous stage for a while. If one partner finds the exercises upsetting, or there's tension in the relationship, a qualified sex therapist can guide you through the program together.
