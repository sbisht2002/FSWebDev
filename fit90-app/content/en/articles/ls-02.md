Alcohol has a long reputation as a social lubricant. A drink can make you feel more relaxed and confident, which is why many men reach for one before a date or intimacy. But the relationship between alcohol and sexual performance is not as friendly as it seems.

## In the moment

Small amounts of alcohol can lower inhibitions and ease nerves. Beyond that, alcohol acts as a depressant on your nervous system. It can:

- make it harder to get or keep an erection
- reduce sensation, which can make it harder to reach orgasm
- dull your judgement about safer sex and consent
- make you sleepy, and reduce your awareness of your partner

There's a well-known phrase for the erection problems that come with heavy drinking: "whisky dick". It's common, temporary, and a direct effect of alcohol rather than a sign that something is permanently wrong.

## Over time

Regular heavy drinking can cause longer-lasting problems:

- **Lower testosterone,** which can reduce desire and energy
- **Nerve damage,** which can affect erections and sensation
- **Liver damage,** which affects hormone balance
- **Weight gain** from alcohol's calories and the snacks that often come with it
- **High blood pressure,** which harms the blood vessels erections depend on
- **Poor sleep,** because alcohol breaks up deep and dream sleep
- **Low mood and anxiety,** especially the day after drinking

## The confidence trap

If you find you need a drink to feel relaxed enough for intimacy, alcohol may have become a "safety behaviour". It brings short-term relief, but it stops you learning that you can cope without it, and it can create the very problems you're worried about. The confidence lessons, especially the one on gentle exposure, can help you build confidence without relying on alcohol.

## How much is too much?

There's no completely safe level of alcohol for health. For sexual health, less is clearly better. If you drink, keep it occasional and moderate, have alcohol-free days every week, and avoid binge drinking. A useful rule: if you're planning intimacy, keep it to one drink or none.

## Practical ways to cut down

- **Count your drinks** for a week. Many people are surprised by the total. The weekly check-in in this app can help you track it.
- **Swap some drinks** for alternatives: soda with lime, alcohol-free beer, buttermilk or a mocktail.
- **Eat before and while drinking,** and alternate alcoholic drinks with water.
- **Set a limit before you start,** and tell a friend so they can help.
- **Plan alcohol-free activities** with friends or your partner.
- **Notice your triggers,** such as stress, boredom or certain people, and plan another response.

## What a "standard drink" looks like

Drink sizes vary a lot, especially when friends pour. Roughly, one standard drink is a small glass of beer (about 330 ml of regular-strength beer), a small glass of wine (about 100 ml), or a single 30 ml peg of spirits like whisky or rum. A large bottle of strong beer can be two or three standard drinks on its own, and a "large peg" is double. Counting honestly is the first step to cutting down.

## The morning after

Even if things went fine the night before, a hangover brings tiredness, poor sleep, low mood and anxiety. Many men notice that their confidence and patience are lower for a day or two after heavy drinking. That's part of the hidden cost.

## When to get help

If you find it hard to cut down, need a drink to get through the day, or drinking is causing problems at work, at home or in your relationship, please talk to a doctor.
