"Mediterranean diet" can sound like olive oil, fish and expensive vegetables. But what makes it healthy isn't the geography. It's the pattern: mostly plants, whole grains, legumes, nuts, healthy fats, some fish or other protein, and not much processed food or sugar. Indian kitchens already have nearly everything needed, often at lower cost.

## Building your plate

A simple guide for main meals:

- **Half the plate: vegetables and salad.** Any sabzi, cooked greens like palak or methi, kachumber salad, cucumber, tomatoes, carrots.
- **A quarter: protein.** Dal, chana, rajma, moong sprouts, soya chunks, eggs, curd, paneer, tofu, fish or chicken.
- **A quarter: whole grains.** Whole-wheat roti, millet roti (jowar, bajra, ragi), brown or hand-pounded rice, or a smaller portion of white rice with extra dal and vegetables.
- **On the side:** a bowl of curd or buttermilk, a little pickle or chutney.

## Smart Indian swaps

- **Maida to atta:** whole-wheat roti or paratha instead of naan or white bread.
- **Refined rice to mixed grains:** mix half white rice with brown rice, or add millets.
- **Deep-fried to roasted:** roasted chana or makhana instead of namkeen; dry-roasted peanuts instead of fried snacks.
- **Ghee and vanaspati to vegetable oils:** cook with mustard, groundnut or rice-bran oil, and keep ghee small. Avoid vanaspati, which can be high in harmful trans fats.
- **Sweet chai to less sugar:** cut the sugar gradually; your taste adjusts within a few weeks.
- **Packaged juice to whole fruit:** eat the fruit and keep the fibre.
- **Biscuits to nuts and fruit:** a handful of peanuts with a banana or guava makes a good snack.

## Protein on a budget

Protein supports muscles and keeps you full. Affordable sources include:

- dal, chana, rajma, lobia and moong (pair them with grains for a complete protein)
- soya chunks, which are very high in protein and cheap
- peanuts and roasted chana
- eggs, if you eat them
- milk, curd and homemade paneer
- small fish such as sardines or mackerel in coastal areas, which also provide healthy omega-3 fats

## Healthy fats

Fat isn't the enemy. The type matters. Nuts, seeds (flax, sesame, pumpkin), mustard or groundnut oil, and oily fish provide fats that support heart health. Limit fats from deep-fried food, vanaspati, processed meats and lots of butter or ghee.

## A sample day (vegetarian)

- **Breakfast:** vegetable poha or upma made with oats, plus a glass of milk or a bowl of curd
- **Lunch:** two whole-wheat or millet rotis, dal, mixed vegetable sabzi, salad, buttermilk
- **Snack:** a guava or orange and a handful of roasted chana or peanuts
- **Dinner:** brown rice or roti, rajma or chana masala, palak or other greens, curd

## Eating out and on the go

Most of us eat outside sometimes. A few easy choices help: pick tandoori or grilled dishes over deep-fried ones, choose dal and roti over biryani with extra oil, ask for less butter on your dal, add a salad, and share desserts. At a dhaba, a thali with dal, sabzi, roti and curd is often one of the healthiest options on the menu.

## Small steps beat big changes

You don't need to change everything at once. Pick one swap a week, such as switching to whole-wheat roti, adding a salad to lunch, or cutting sugar in tea. Small changes you keep are worth far more than a perfect diet you abandon after a fortnight.

If you have diabetes, heart disease or kidney disease, please talk to your doctor or a registered dietitian before making big changes to your diet, as your needs may be different. This app never recommends supplements.
