If you smoke, stopping is one of the most powerful things you can do for your erections, as well as your heart, lungs and overall health. Smoking damages the blood vessels that erections depend on, and the effect is strongest the more you smoke and the longer you've smoked.

## How smoking affects erections

An erection needs the arteries in the penis to open up and fill with blood. Tobacco smoke:

- damages the lining of blood vessels, reducing the nitric oxide that helps them relax and open
- narrows and stiffens arteries over time
- raises blood pressure and heart rate in the short term
- reduces the amount of oxygen your blood can carry

A review that combined data from many studies found that smokers were more likely to have erectile dysfunction than non-smokers, with risk rising the more cigarettes a man smoked. Because the arteries in the penis are small, they may show damage earlier than those around the heart.

## What about bidis, chewing tobacco and gutka?

Bidis are not a safer choice. They deliver high levels of nicotine, tar and carbon monoxide. Smokeless tobacco such as gutka, khaini and paan with tobacco also contains nicotine and other harmful chemicals, and causes mouth cancers. Nicotine in any form narrows blood vessels.

## And vaping?

E-cigarettes and vapes usually contain nicotine, which narrows blood vessels and raises heart rate. Research on vaping and erections is still developing, but there's reason for caution. Vaping may help some people quit cigarettes, but the healthiest goal is to become free from nicotine altogether.

## The good news: quitting helps

Blood vessels begin to recover after you stop. Within weeks, circulation starts to improve. Some studies have found that men who quit smoking report improvements in erectile function, especially younger men and those with less damage to begin with. Quitting also lowers your risk of heart disease, stroke and many cancers, and improves breathing, stamina, taste and smell.

## How to quit

Quitting is hard, and most people take a few attempts. Each attempt teaches you something. These steps raise your chances:

- **Set a quit date** within the next two weeks, and tell people who'll support you.
- **Know your triggers:** tea, meals, stress, friends who smoke. Plan what you'll do instead.
- **Use support:** stop-smoking services and quit lines roughly double or triple your chances compared with going it alone. In India, the National Tobacco Quitline is 1800-112-356.
- **Ask a doctor about medicines** and nicotine replacement such as patches or gum, which reduce cravings and are much safer than smoking.
- **Ride out cravings:** they usually pass within a few minutes. Try slow breathing, a short walk, drinking water or chewing something.
- **Use the money you save** for something you enjoy.
- **If you slip,** don't give up. Work out what triggered it and keep going.

## Coping with the first few weeks

The first two weeks are usually the hardest. You may feel irritable, restless, hungry or low, and find it hard to concentrate. These are withdrawal symptoms, and they ease with time. Regular meals, plenty of water, exercise and enough sleep all help. Many men find that a short walk or a few minutes of slow breathing is enough to get through a craving.

Some people worry about weight gain after quitting. A little weight gain is common, but the health benefits of quitting far outweigh it, and the eating and exercise lessons in this app can help you manage it.

## Track your progress

Log smoke-free days in your weekly check-in. Many men find that watching the numbers improve, alongside better breathing and stamina, keeps them motivated.
