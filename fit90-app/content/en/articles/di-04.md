Eating well is as much about what you eat less of as what you add. This lesson covers the foods and habits most worth cutting back on, plus the often-forgotten role of water. There are no strict rules here: small, steady changes add up.

## Ultra-processed food

Ultra-processed foods are factory-made products with long ingredient lists: packaged snacks, instant noodles, sugary cereals, biscuits, processed meats, many ready-to-eat foods and soft drinks. They tend to be high in refined starch, sugar, salt and unhealthy fats, and low in fibre. Diets high in them are linked with weight gain, type 2 diabetes and heart disease, all of which affect erections.

**Try:** keeping packaged snacks out of easy reach, cooking simple meals in bulk, and replacing one packaged snack a day with fruit, roasted chana or peanuts.

## Added sugar

Sugary drinks, sweets, mithai and sweetened tea can spike blood sugar and add calories without filling you up. Over time, high blood sugar damages the nerves and small blood vessels that erections depend on.

**Try:** cutting the sugar in tea and coffee by half for two weeks, then half again; swapping soft drinks and packaged juice for water, buttermilk, lemon water or plain soda; and keeping sweets for occasions.

## Too much salt

High salt intake raises blood pressure, and high blood pressure damages blood vessels. Pickles, papads, namkeen, instant noodles and many restaurant dishes are very salty.

**Try:** tasting before adding salt, using lemon, herbs and spices for flavour, and keeping pickles and papads small.

## Alcohol

Alcohol deserves its own lesson, but in short: drinking more than a little can make erections harder in the moment, and heavy drinking over time can lower testosterone and damage nerves and blood vessels. Less is better for erections, sleep, mood and weight.

## Weight and belly fat

Fat stored around the belly is particularly linked with blood-sugar problems, lower testosterone and blood-vessel damage. In a two-year trial, men with obesity who lost weight through healthier eating and more exercise had better erectile function than those who didn't. You don't need to aim for a "perfect" body: losing even 5 to 10 percent of your weight, if you're carrying extra, can improve blood sugar, blood pressure and erections.

This program doesn't set calorie targets. If you want personal targets for losing fat or building muscle, the Fitness 90 program offers them separately.

## Blood sugar

Diabetes is one of the most common physical causes of erection problems. If you have diabetes, keeping your blood sugar in your target range protects nerves and blood vessels. If you've never been checked and you're overweight, have a family history of diabetes or feel very thirsty or tired, ask a doctor for a simple blood test. Type 2 diabetes is very common in India and often goes unnoticed for years.

## Hydration

Mild dehydration can leave you tired, headachy and less energetic. Drinking enough water also helps when you're exercising more. Most adults need roughly 2 to 3 litres of fluid a day, more in hot weather or with sweaty exercise. A simple guide is pale yellow urine. The water tracker in the Fitness program can help if you tend to forget.

## One change at a time

Pick the change that feels easiest and stick with it for two weeks before adding another. People who make one or two lasting changes usually do better than those who try to overhaul everything at once and burn out.

If you have diabetes, heart disease or kidney disease, please talk to your doctor or a registered dietitian before making big changes to your diet. This app never recommends supplements.
