Talking about early ejaculation (PE) or erection difficulties (ED) can feel like one of the hardest conversations you'll have. Many men stay silent for months or years, worrying about being judged. But silence often makes things harder: partners may assume the problem is them, that you've lost interest, or that something else is wrong. An honest, calm conversation usually brings relief on both sides.

## Before the conversation

- **Choose a good time:** relaxed, private, not in bed and not straight after a difficult moment. A walk or a quiet evening at home often works well.
- **Know your main message:** you care about them, this is something you're working on, and you'd like to tackle it together.
- **Expect some nerves.** That's normal. You don't need perfect words, and it's fine to read from a note or say that you're nervous.

## Words you could use

Opening the conversation:

- "There's something I've been wanting to talk about. It's a bit awkward for me, but it's important."
- "I've noticed I've been really anxious about sex lately, and I think it's affecting things. I want you to know it's not about you."

About early ejaculation:

- "I often finish sooner than I'd like, and it's been worrying me. I've started doing some exercises to help, and I'd love for us to work on it together."

About erections:

- "Sometimes I lose my erection, and I've been stressing about it. I'm planning to see a doctor to rule out anything physical, and I'm also working on the anxiety side."

Inviting your partner in:

- "How has it been for you? I'd really like to know what you feel and what you enjoy."
- "Would you be up for trying some things together, like taking it slower and focusing more on touch for a while?"

## Listening matters as much as talking

Your partner may have feelings of their own: relief, confusion, worry that it was about them, or even frustration that it took a while to talk. Let them share without getting defensive. Thank them for listening. This is a conversation between teammates.

## Reassure them

Many partners worry that problems with erections or timing mean you're not attracted to them. Say clearly that it isn't about them: "I find you really attractive. This is about my nerves and my body, not about you."

## Make a plan together

Ideas you might agree on:

- taking intercourse off the table for a few weeks and trying sensate focus
- slowing down and adding more touch and foreplay
- your partner helping with stop-start or squeeze practice
- agreeing on a simple signal for pausing
- you seeing a doctor, and perhaps going to a qualified sex therapist together

## Following up

One conversation rarely covers everything. Check in again a week or two later: "How have you been feeling about what we talked about?" Share any progress you've made, such as practising techniques or booking a doctor's appointment. Keeping the conversation going shows that you're committed, and it makes future talks easier and more natural for both of you.

## If the conversation doesn't go well

Sometimes a partner reacts with hurt or frustration, especially if the problem has been affecting them for a long time. Give it time, and try again later. A couples counsellor or sex therapist can help if talking keeps getting stuck.
