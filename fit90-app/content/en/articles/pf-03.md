Most advice about pelvic-floor exercises is about squeezing. But a healthy muscle isn't just strong. It can also relax fully when it needs to. Think of your hand: a fist that can't open isn't useful, however strong it is. Your pelvic floor works the same way.

## Strong isn't the same as healthy

A pelvic floor that is always partly tight is sometimes called "overactive" or "hypertonic". It can happen with stress, anxiety, long periods of sitting, or after pain. People often don't notice it, because it simply feels normal to them.

An overactive pelvic floor can contribute to:

- pain in the pelvis, perineum, testicles or penis
- discomfort after ejaculation
- feeling that you need to urinate often, or difficulty starting
- difficulty relaxing during sex, which can make anxiety worse

So more squeezing is not always the answer. For some men, learning to let go is the most important part of training.

## What is a reverse Kegel?

A reverse Kegel, which this app calls a "drop", is the opposite of a squeeze. Instead of lifting the pelvic floor, you gently let it soften and lower.

It is **not** pushing or straining. It's more like the feeling of letting go when you start to urinate, or the softening you might notice when you take a slow, deep breath into your belly.

## How to practise a drop

1. Lie on your back with your knees bent, or sit comfortably.
2. Put one hand on your belly.
3. Breathe in slowly through your nose, letting your belly rise.
4. As you breathe in, imagine the space between your sitting bones widening, and let your pelvic floor gently drop and soften.
5. Breathe out slowly and let everything settle. Don't squeeze.
6. Repeat five to ten times.

Your breathing muscle (the diaphragm) and your pelvic floor move together. When you breathe in, the diaphragm lowers and the pelvic floor naturally lowers a little too. When you breathe out, both rise slightly. Slow belly breathing helps the pelvic floor relax on its own.

## Why this helps with lasting longer

When arousal builds, many men tense up without noticing: belly, buttocks, thighs and pelvic floor. That tension can speed things up. Being able to notice tension and let it go is a useful skill for control. The last-longer lessons build on it, pairing slow breathing with gentle drops.

## Why this helps with anxiety

Anxiety tends to show up in the body as tight shoulders, a tight jaw, shallow breathing and a tight pelvic floor. Practising drops with slow breathing gives your body a clear signal that it's safe to settle. Over time, many people find they can do this quickly whenever they notice tension.

## Noticing hidden tension

A few times a day, pause and scan: jaw, shoulders, belly, buttocks, pelvic floor. Are any of them gripping? Take one slow breath in, let the pelvic floor soften as you do, and breathe out. Many men are surprised how often they find tension they hadn't noticed. Catching it early is a skill you can build.

## Balance in your sessions

Each trainer session in this app includes:

- **Squeezes and holds** to build strength and endurance
- **Full releases** after every squeeze, at least as long as the hold
- **Drops** at the end, to finish relaxed

If you have pain or high anxiety, the app gives you a **relaxation-first** version with softer, shorter squeezes and twice as many drops.

## When to get help

If you often have pelvic pain, pain with ejaculation, or urinary symptoms, please see a doctor. A pelvic-floor physiotherapist can assess whether your muscles are too tight, too weak, or both, and teach you the right balance. Stop squeezing exercises if they make any pain worse.
