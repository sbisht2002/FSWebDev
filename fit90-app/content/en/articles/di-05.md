Walk through any market or scroll online and you'll see products promising stronger erections, more stamina or instant results: herbal capsules, "power" powders, special oils, and lists of aphrodisiac foods. It's understandable to be tempted. Here's what the evidence actually says.

## Aphrodisiac foods

Oysters, chocolate, saffron, honey, chillies, figs and many other foods have reputations as aphrodisiacs. For most of them, there's little or no good-quality evidence that they directly improve erections or stamina. Some of the reputation comes from tradition, shape or symbolism rather than science.

What does have evidence is the overall eating pattern: plenty of vegetables, fruit, whole grains, legumes and nuts, with less processed food and sugar. That supports your blood vessels over months and years. No single food works like a switch.

The one possible exception is the broad group of flavonoid-rich foods such as berries and citrus, which have been linked with a lower risk of erection problems in a large study. But even there, the effect comes from regular eating over years, not from a single meal.

## "Sexual enhancement" supplements

This is where real caution is needed. Supplements sold for sexual performance are not tested or regulated in the same way as medicines. Problems found with these products include:

- **Hidden prescription drugs.** Regulators such as the U.S. Food and Drug Administration have repeatedly found "natural" or "herbal" sexual enhancement products containing undeclared prescription medicines, sometimes at high or unpredictable doses.
- **Dangerous interactions.** Some hidden drugs can cause a dangerous drop in blood pressure when combined with heart medicines called nitrates, often prescribed for chest pain.
- **Heavy metals and contaminants** in some poorly made products.
- **No proven benefit** for many common herbal ingredients.
- **Delayed diagnosis.** Relying on supplements can delay seeing a doctor, when erection problems might be an early sign of heart disease or diabetes.

For these reasons, **this app never recommends supplements**, and its coach won't either.

## Online "delay" sprays and creams

Some numbing sprays and creams for early ejaculation are real medical treatments that a doctor may prescribe. Others sold online have unknown contents. If you're interested in this kind of treatment, talk to a doctor, who can recommend a properly regulated product and explain how to use it safely.

## What actually works

- Heart-healthy eating and regular exercise
- Not smoking, and drinking little or no alcohol
- Good sleep and stress management
- Pelvic-floor training and behavioural techniques
- Working on anxiety and communication
- Medical treatments prescribed by a doctor, which are effective and safe for most men when used correctly

If you have erection problems or early ejaculation that bothers you, a doctor can discuss proven, regulated treatments. These conversations are routine for doctors, and you can bring up anything you've been tempted to try.

## Why these products are so tempting

Sexual worries can feel private and embarrassing, and a product you can order quietly online seems easier than talking to a doctor. Marketing plays on exactly that feeling. It helps to remember that doctors discuss erections and ejaculation every day, that effective treatments exist, and that a check-up can also catch early signs of diabetes or heart disease. The quiet option isn't always the safe one.

## A note on traditional remedies

Many traditional systems of medicine include remedies for sexual health. Some herbs are being studied, but the evidence is generally weak, and quality and contents can vary a lot between products. If you choose to use a traditional remedy, buy from a regulated source, tell your doctor, and never combine it with prescription medicines without advice.

## Spotting a dodgy product

Be suspicious of anything that promises instant results, is "100% natural" with "no side effects", uses words like "miracle" or "secret formula", is sold only online with no clear manufacturer, or is marketed with dramatic before-and-after claims. If in doubt, leave it out.
