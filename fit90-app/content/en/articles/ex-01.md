If you could pick only one lifestyle change to support your erections, regular aerobic exercise would be a strong candidate. Erections depend on healthy blood vessels and good blood flow, and few things improve blood vessels as reliably as getting your heart rate up regularly.

## Erections are a blood-vessel event

An erection happens when the arteries in the penis widen and the spongy tissue fills with blood. That depends on the inner lining of your blood vessels, called the endothelium, releasing a chemical called nitric oxide that tells the vessels to relax and open.

The same lining runs through every artery in your body. Things that damage it, such as smoking, high blood pressure, high blood sugar, high cholesterol, inactivity and excess belly fat, also affect erections. Because the arteries in the penis are small, they often show trouble earlier than the larger arteries around the heart. Large reviews have found that men with erectile dysfunction have a higher risk of future heart disease. That's why erection problems are worth mentioning to a doctor, and why looking after your heart looks after your sex life.

## What exercise does

Regular aerobic exercise:

- helps the endothelium release more nitric oxide, so blood vessels open more easily
- lowers blood pressure and improves blood sugar control
- improves cholesterol levels
- helps reduce belly fat
- lifts mood and lowers stress and anxiety
- improves sleep, which supports healthy testosterone levels
- builds stamina and body confidence

## How much is enough?

A 2018 systematic review of exercise studies in men with erection problems concluded that about **40 minutes of moderate to vigorous aerobic exercise, four times a week, for six months** helped reduce erection problems. That's about **160 minutes a week**. The men in those studies had erection problems linked to inactivity, obesity, high blood pressure, metabolic syndrome or heart disease, so the benefits may be biggest if any of those apply to you.

This lines up well with general health guidance from the World Health Organization: 150 to 300 minutes of moderate activity a week, plus muscle-strengthening on two or more days.

## What counts as moderate or vigorous?

- **Moderate:** you're breathing harder and feel warm, but can still talk in full sentences. Brisk walking, easy cycling, swimming, dancing.
- **Vigorous:** you can only say a few words before needing a breath. Jogging, running, fast cycling, skipping, sports like football or badminton played hard.

You don't need a gym. Brisk walking is one of the best places to start, and the walking plan in this app builds you up gradually.

## How long until it helps?

Improvements in blood-vessel function begin within weeks, but in studies the clearer benefits for erections came after around three to six months of steady exercise. Think of this as a long-term investment. It also helps your mood, energy and confidence far sooner than that.

## Strength training counts too

Muscle-strengthening exercise twice a week supports healthy body composition, blood-sugar control and confidence. The Fitness 90 program in this app gives you a home calisthenics plan, and you can add pelvic-floor squeezes during exercises like bridges and squats.

## Before you start

Exercise is safe for most people, but please check with a doctor first if you have chest pain or tightness with exertion, a known heart condition, very high or uncontrolled blood pressure, or if you get faint, dizzy or very breathless with light activity. Start gently and build up slowly. If you feel chest pain while exercising, stop and get medical help.

## Combine it

Exercise works best alongside the other pieces: not smoking, sensible alcohol, good sleep, heart-healthy food and managing stress. Each one helps the others. If you have erection problems despite healthy habits, a doctor can check for other causes and discuss effective treatments, which can be used alongside everything in this app.
