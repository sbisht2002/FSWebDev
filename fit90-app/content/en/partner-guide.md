# The Partner's Guide

*For the partner of someone working on lasting longer, erections or performance anxiety.*

Thank you for reading this. Your partner shared it because they care about you and about your time together. This short guide explains what they're working on and how you can help, if you'd like to.

## What's going on

**Early ejaculation** (finishing sooner than wanted) and **erection difficulties** are very common. They are rarely about attraction. They are usually about nerves, stress, the body's reflexes, health, or a mix of these. **Performance anxiety**, worrying about "doing it right", makes both more likely, which can create a frustrating loop.

Your partner is using an app with pelvic-floor exercises, breathing, behavioural techniques and lessons on anxiety and communication. Many men find these help, especially with patience and support. Some will also see a doctor, because effective medical treatments exist and some causes need a check-up.

## How you can help

- **Reassure them it isn't about you,** and that you find them attractive, if that's how you feel. Many men worry silently about this.
- **Take the pressure off.** Intimacy doesn't need to be about how long intercourse lasts. Closeness, touch and fun count.
- **Be patient with progress.** Good weeks and harder weeks are both normal.
- **Use humour kindly.** Laughing together, not at each other, eases tension.
- **Share what you enjoy.** Your pleasure matters too, and knowing what you like helps them relax.
- **Avoid blame or comparison,** and avoid checking or commenting on performance in the moment.
- **Look after yourself.** Your feelings, including frustration, are valid. Talk about them at a calm time.

## Trying sensate focus together

Sensate focus is a set of touch exercises widely used by sex therapists. For a few weeks, you agree to set aside intercourse and orgasm, and take turns exploring touch, focusing on sensation rather than goals.

1. **Stage 1:** take turns touching each other's bodies, avoiding breasts and genitals. Notice textures and temperature.
2. **Stage 2:** whole-body touch, including genitals as part of the whole, still with no goal.
3. **Stage 3:** the receiver guides the toucher's hand to show what they enjoy.
4. **Stage 4:** touching each other at the same time, still relaxed and goal-free.

Talk afterwards about what you noticed and enjoyed. Either of you can pause or stop at any time. Move on only when you both feel ready.

## Helping with techniques

If you're both comfortable, your partner may ask you to help with the "stop-start" or "squeeze" techniques: pausing when they signal, then continuing once they've settled. A simple agreed signal, like a word or a touch on the hand, keeps it easy.

## When to suggest a doctor

Gently encourage a doctor's visit if problems started suddenly, happen most of the time, or come with pain, diabetes, high blood pressure or heart problems. Erection problems can be an early sign of heart disease, so a check-up protects their health too. A qualified sex therapist can help couples work through anxiety and communication together.
